micro-PROLOG Backtracked resurrects the classic ZX Spectrum micro-PROLOG and SIMPLE workflows for the modern web. By moving the environment to the browser, this implementation bypasses the severe memory constraints of 1980s hardware.

This implementation was created as a fan project without access to the original micro-PROLOG source code; consequently, its behavior and internal logic may differ from the 1982 original.

About micro-PROLOG

Developed in the late 1970s at Imperial College London and later distributed by Logic Programming Associates (LPA), micro-PROLOG was a pioneering adaptation of logic programming for 8-bit microcomputers. Its unique feature was its high level of accessibility; it replaced complex formal logic syntax with a SIMPLE interface that used English-like structures, making it intuitive for non-programmers. Historically, it was a cornerstone of the UK’s Microelectronics Education Programme, serving as the first introduction to Artificial Intelligence and symbolic reasoning for an entire generation of students. By shrinking a language that previously required mainframe power to fit on home computers, it proved that sophisticated computational logic could be a universal educational tool.
